Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about how this transcript tool works and what to expect.

1. Is this tool really free?

Yes. You can fetch, copy and download transcripts without paying anything and without creating an account. The site is supported by ads, which is how the free service stays running.

2. Which videos work with this tool?

Any public YouTube video that has captions — either uploaded by the creator or auto-generated by YouTube. Normal videos, Shorts, live streams (after they finish) and music videos with captions all work. Private videos and age-restricted videos cannot be fetched.

3. The tool says "no captions". What does that mean?

It means the video has no subtitle track at all, so there is no spoken-text data to convert into a transcript. This is a limitation of the video itself, not of the tool — no website can produce an accurate transcript for such a video without listening to the audio.

4. Can I get a transcript in Hindi?

Yes — if the video has a Hindi caption track (many Indian channels and TED-style talks do), pick Hindi in the language list. You can also use the Translate option to convert another language's captions into Hindi. Auto-translated and auto-generated captions may contain mistakes.

5. What is the difference between .txt and .srt downloads?

The .txt file is readable text with timestamps, good for notes and reading. The .srt file is a subtitle file with timing codes, made for video editors and for uploading subtitles to your own videos.

6. Why do some transcripts have mistakes?

Auto-generated captions are produced by speech-recognition software. It can mishear names, technical words and fast speech. Captions uploaded by the creator ("manual") are usually accurate. Always proofread before using a transcript for anything important.

7. Is it legal to download a transcript of someone else's video?

Reading, studying and quoting short parts with credit is normal practice, but the words in a video belong to its creator. Do not republish full transcripts as your own content or use them commercially without the creator's permission. When in doubt, treat a transcript like the video itself — it is someone else's work.

8. Do you store the videos or links I use?

No accounts, no uploads. The link you paste is used only to fetch that video's captions, and results are briefly cached on the server so repeat requests are fast. See the Privacy Policy for the full details.

9. Sometimes it says YouTube blocked the fetch. Why?

YouTube limits how often caption files can be requested from one server. When that happens, waiting a minute and pressing Try Again almost always fixes it. Trying a different caption language can help too.

10. Can I get a transcript of my own video?

Yes, and it is one of the best uses: download your own transcript to write video descriptions, chapters, pinned comments and blog versions of your videos.
